Most sales on Pluribus go well, between people who meet and see the item. These simple rules keep away the few who cheat.

When you buy

  • Meet in a busy public place, in daylight: a café, a square, outside a shop. For expensive items, don’t go alone.
  • See the item before you pay, and try it out: the phone, the laptop, the car.
  • Don’t send money in advance, not even “to reserve it” or “for the delivery”: pay when the item is in your hands. Anyone who rushes you, or asks to be paid in gift cards, cryptocurrency or transfers abroad, is scamming you.
  • A price far below the market usually means the item doesn’t exist.
  • Don’t follow links to “secure” payment pages outside Pluribus.

When you sell

  • Meet in a public place too, and don’t let strangers into your home unless you have to.
  • Accept cash in hand, or a transfer you can see has arrived in your account. A “payment confirmation” sent as a photo or an email can be fake.
  • Beware of buyers who offer more than the price and ask for the difference back, or who send “their courier”.

Cars and property

  • Cars: check the ownership documents and the chassis number, take the car to a mechanic you choose, and transfer the ownership at a notary. Don’t pay before that.
  • Property: see it yourself, check who owns it with the ownership certificate from the cadastre, and don’t pay a deposit without a written contract.

Jobs

  • A real employer never asks you to pay for the application, the training or “the paperwork”.
  • Don’t send copies of your personal documents before you’re sure the offer is real.

Your account

  • The sign-in code is for you alone: Pluribus never asks you for it by phone or message. Whoever asks you for it wants to get into your account.
  • Don’t share bank details, copies of documents or passwords in messages.

When something goes wrong

  • Press “Report” on the listing’s page: the moderators look at it, and many reports take the listing down at once. Once you’ve reported it, “Block seller” takes the seller off your pages.
  • If you’ve been scammed, go to the police (129) and your bank as soon as you can.
